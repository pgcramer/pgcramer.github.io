Relativistic Blackbody Star

A star moving at relativistic speed, as a camera would record it. The Doppler shift changes its colour, relativistic beaming changes its brightness, and both follow from one rule: a blackbody at temperature T, seen with Doppler factor D, is an exact blackbody at temperature DT. Change the detector band to see the same star in ultraviolet, infrared or X-rays.

For a source at position P moving with velocity V (units of c), the Doppler factor is D = 1 / [γ(1 + v_r)] with v_r = V·P/|P| (positive when receding), and light emitted at wavelength λ arrives at λ/D. Because Iν/ν3 is a Lorentz invariant, a Planck spectrum at T is observed as a Planck spectrum at DT, so the bolometric flux scales as D4.

The detector band is split into three equal parts in log λ; each channel integrates the observed spectrum over its part. With "exposure relative to the Sun in this band" on, exposure 1 means a stationary Sun at 10 light-years just reaches full brightness in the chosen band. With it off, the reference is always the Sun in visible light, so different bands can be compared directly.
